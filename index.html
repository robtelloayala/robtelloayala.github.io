    Jose Roberto Tello Ayala

    Interpretable machine learning for clinical decision-making.

    PhD Candidate in Applied Mathematics

    Data to Actionable Knowledge Lab

    Harvard John A. Paulson School of Engineering and Applied Sciences

    I am a fifth-year PhD candidate in Applied Mathematics at Harvard University, advised by Finale Doshi-Velez. I develop interpretable machine-learning methods for clinical decision-making, with applications in cardiovascular imaging and precision psychiatry. I am part of Harvard’s Data to Actionable Knowledge Lab and the Fahed Lab at the Broad Institute and Massachusetts General Hospital.

    Research Themes

    Interpretability and model auditing

    I develop methods to understand what predictive models learn and how their information compares with expert knowledge, including Signature Activation and Shared Canonical Component Removal.

    Cardiovascular imaging

    I use machine learning to extract measurable features from coronary angiography and study their relationship with disease, including coronary artery tortuosity.

    Precision psychiatry

    I study factors associated with antidepressant treatment dropout and investigate what predictive models can reveal beyond expert-defined clinical features.
